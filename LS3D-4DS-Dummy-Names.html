Mafia · 4DS reference

Every frame name the game looks for

The names Mafia matches when it loads a model — on vehicles, characters, weapons and in the city — with the exact spelling each one needs, what happens when it is wrong, and the names that look meaningful but nothing reads. The text each frame carries is a subject of its own: see 4DS User Properties.

Axes and counts

Directions are given in Mafia’s axes — X right, Y up, Z forward — with Blender’s in brackets where it matters (Blender shows Mafia’s Y as Z and Z as Y). A frame’s facing is its local +Z, which is +Y on the object in Blender.

Counts outside the vehicle part come from every .4ds shipped: 3,206 files, the models folder and the mission scenes together. “Shipped” counts in the vehicle part come from the 440 models in the game’s models folder that have both a BODY and a CENTRE; that includes cutscene copies, and 54 of them have no wheels at all.

Part one · Frame names

How names are matched

The rules below apply to every name in this part unless an entry says otherwise. Most of the surprises in vehicle modding come from the second one.

RuleWhat it means for you
Own name onlyThe game puts the model’s name in front of every frame when it loads (polimFor00.BODY). Lookups ignore that part, so you name the frame just BODY.
Prefix patternsA name shown with a trailing * matches anything starting with it. WH* takes WHL0 — and also WHEELCAP, which then fails the wheel checks and stops the car loading. DOOR* turns DOORHANDLE into a door. Keep unrelated parts away from these prefixes.
CaseIgnored for name lookups (body works), except the wheel parents DWHL/DWHR, which must be upper case.
Exact namesNames shown without * must match in full. HEAD is found; HEAD01 is not.
Frame typeEach lookup only considers certain types (the chips on each entry). A SEAT0 that is a mesh instead of a dummy is simply not seen.
NumbersRead as a whole number right where the entry says: DOOR1 and DOOR01 are both door 1. Missing digits read as 0.
One of manyWhere a single frame is wanted and several match, BODY, CENTRE, HEAD, SWHEEL, SMOKE_MOTOR, AI00 and AI01 take the last one in the hierarchy; FUEL and TANK take the first.
Where it is measuredSome helper positions are taken as if their parent were BODY, others from the model’s origin. Each entry says which; put the frame there or it lands in the wrong place.
Removed on loadHelper dummies marked removed are read and then deleted from the car, so they cost nothing while driving and cannot be animated or looked up later.

Putting a car together

What every name has to be, where it has to sit, and what it takes with it. The entries below this one give each name in full; this is the shape they make together.

What becomes what

The 4DS frame type is what the lookups test, and in Blender that is the Frame Type field in the 4DS Object panel — not the kind of Blender object. In practice an empty is a Dummy, its box drawn from the empty's size, and a mesh is a Visual · Object. A name looked for as a dummy never finds a mesh, and the other way round: this is the single most common reason a car loads with a part missing and no message about it.

Where the names sit

Every lookup starts at the model's own root and walks the whole tree, so most names may sit anywhere — with two exceptions: BALLAST* is looked for only beneath BODY, and WINDOW* only beneath its own door. Where a frame's position is measured from is a separate question, and each entry says which: some are read as if their parent were BODY, others from the model's origin.

What goes wrong, and why

What you didWhat the game does
A seat, head or wheel mount modeled as a meshNever found. Those names are looked for as dummies only, and nothing is reported.
A door or steering wheel made an emptyNever found. Doors, windows, strips, axles and SWHEEL are looked for as meshes only.
A part named WHEELCAP or DOORHANDLECaught by WH* and DOOR*. The cap then fails the wheel checks and the car refuses to load; the handle becomes a door that opens.
Wheel mounts named dwhl0Not matched. The wheel parents are the one lookup that is case-sensitive; everything else ignores case.
Five wheels, or threeRefused. Wheels come in pairs, at least four, each one a child of a DWHL/DWHR mount.
Two frames with the same nameOne is used and the other ignored. BODY, CENTRE, HEAD, SWHEEL, SMOKE_MOTOR, AI00 and AI01 take the last one in the tree; FUEL and TANK take the first.
An animation on a helper dummyNothing moves. The dummies marked Removed are read once and deleted as the car loads.
DOOR01 beside DOOR1Both are door 1. The number is read as a number, so leading zeros make no difference and a gap in the numbering is a gap in the car.
A car with no CENTRE, no SEAT0 or no AI pointsThe car does not load at all. Those, with BODY and four wheels, are the set the game insists on.

Body, wheels and axles

BODY

Dummy or objectRequired

The car’s frame of reference. HEAD, CENTRE, EXHAUST, SMOKE_MOTOR, AI00 and AI01 are all measured in its space, and BALLAST dummies are only looked for beneath it.

Shipped: 440 models, always a dummy

CENTRE

DummyRequiredRemoved

Its box is the car’s main collision box — what other cars, people and objects bump into (shrunk 0.1 inward on each side), plus a second copy reaching 0.5 lower. The centre of mass is placed inside this box by the percentages in the vehicle table. Child of BODY.

Shipped: 440 models

WH* under DWHL* / DWHR*

Object or dummyRequired
WHprefix
Lside: L l R r
0axle, 0 = front

The wheel itself. The character right after WH must be a letter or the car is refused; L/l is left, R/r is right, and any other letter leaves the wheel without a place on the car. The number is the axle, counting from 0 at the front, and every axle needs one wheel on each side.

Its parent must be a dummy whose name starts with DWHL or DWHR in upper case (either one; it need not agree with the wheel’s side). That dummy is the suspension point — it moves up and down, and leans when a single axle arm drives it — while the wheel spins about X inside it. The rolling radius is half the wheel’s height and the default skid-mark width is its width along X.

A [number] on the DWH dummy sets the skid-mark width — see wheel trail width.

Shipped: 386 models · 1,558 wheels, all objects383 models have 4, one has 8, one has 16

AXIS*

ObjectOptional
AXISprefix
L0 solid axle; L or R arm (upper case)
0axle
Aanything, to tell arms apart

Suspension parts the game points at the wheels every frame while the camera is close. Model each one lying along its local +Z (Blender +Y) exactly one unit long: the game aims that axis at its target and stretches it to the distance.

  • AXIS0<n> — a solid axle. Placed at the right wheel of axle n and stretched to the left one; both wheels lean with it.
  • AXISL<n>… / AXISR<n>… — an arm that stays at its own pivot and reaches for that side’s wheel. If it is the only arm on that side of the axle, the wheel leans with it (a swing axle); with two or more (AXISL0A, AXISL0B) the wheel stays upright.
  • An axle number past the last axle is ignored.

An [x y z] property shifts the aiming point — see axle aim offset.

Shipped: 229 models · 242 solid axles, 94 arms (AXISL0A, AXISR1B…)

STRIPL* · STRIPR* · STRIPML/MR* · STRIPFL/FR* · STRIPBL/BR*

ObjectOptional
STRIPprefix
MM, F, B or none
Lside
0axle

Caterpillar tracks. On every strip of a side the texture scrolls with engine speed. The lettered ones are also stretched to follow the wheels:

  • STRIPL / STRIPR — scroll only, never move.
  • STRIPML<n> / STRIPMR<n> — the ground run from the bottom of wheel n to the bottom of wheel n+1.
  • STRIPFL<n> / STRIPFR<n> — the front run, from the strip’s own pivot to the bottom of wheel n.
  • STRIPBL<n> / STRIPBR<n> — the back run, the same toward the rear.

Stretching happens along local Z (Blender Y), but the resting length is taken from the strip’s size along X. Only plain objects get the scrolling texture.

Shipped: 2 models (bull00, panz)

SWHEEL

ObjectOptional

The steering wheel. It turns about its own facing direction as the driver steers, while the camera is close. A dummy with this name is ignored.

Shipped: 383 models (one is a dummy and does nothing)

Doors, seats and camera

SEAT*

DummyAt least oneRemoved
SEATprefix
0seat, 0 = driver

Where a character sits and which way they face. Seat n is paired with door n. The seat’s side (positive X is the right side) picks the side characters get in from.

Shipped: 439 models · 184 with one seat, 173 with two, 82 with four

SM<n>* or LG<n>*

DummyPer seatRemoved

The spot a character walks to before getting into seat n. SM uses the front-seat get-in animations, LG the rear-seat ones; SM is tried first. A seat with neither cannot be entered.

Both are prefixes, so SM1 also matches SM10 — on a car with ten or more seats, name carefully.

Shipped: SM in 137 models, LG in 246

DOOR*

ObjectOptional
DOORprefix
0matches seat

A door that swings open about its vertical axis, up to about 72°, when someone gets in or out of the matching seat. Which way it swings depends on the side of the car it is on.

  • An I in its property text flips the swing — see door hinge flag.
  • If the door’s facing points backward (Mafia −Z), characters use the get-in animations for a seat without a door to open.
  • Doors on shipped cars also carry [DEFORM BODY] so they dent.
Shipped: 357 models · 697 doors

WINDOW*

ObjectOptional

A door window, looked for beneath a door. It slides 0.5 units down and hides once fully open. A WINDOW anywhere else is not treated as one. Shipped windows are [DEFORM GLASS] so they crack and break.

Shipped: 108 models · 304 under doors, 14 under BODY that do nothing

HEAD

DummyOptionalRemoved

Where the camera sits in the first-person driving view, looking straight ahead along the car. Child of BODY. Without it the camera sits at the car’s origin.

Shipped: 386 models (their [4,1]-style properties are not read)

Collision and damage

BODYCOL*

DummyOptionalRemoved
BODYCOLprefix
Bbox; else sphere
Mkeep in model
0anything

The volumes the body crashes with. Each one sweeps along with the car; when it touches something, its child dummies are tested as small spheres to find the actual contact points that dent the body and deliver the impact.

  • The letter right after BODYCOL: B makes a box from the dummy’s bounds; anything else makes a sphere whose radius is half the box’s height.
  • Child dummies not named BODYCOL… are the contact spheres (radius: half their height). A BODYCOL under another BODYCOL becomes a smaller volume inside it.
  • An M right after BODYCOL or BODYCOLB keeps that volume and everything under it in the model instead of removing it.
Shipped: 440 models · 946 boxes, 40 spheres, none with M

SPECCOLL*

Dummy or objectOptionalRemoved

Extra collision boxes that move with the car, taken from each dummy’s bounds, in addition to the CENTRE box.

Shipped: 4 truck models, 18 boxes

TANK

DummyOptionalRemoved

The fuel tank: its position, and a radius of half its box’s height. Hits passing within that radius wear the tank down until the car catches fire. Without it the tank cannot be hit. Measured from the model’s origin, so keep it at the top level rather than under BODY.

Shipped: 363 models · 17 of them under BODY
Dents, glass and parts that fall off

Deformation is not set by a frame name but by the [DEFORM …] property on each mesh. Lights and doors tie into it: a light breaks with the deformable part it belongs to.

Lights

A car light is not a Light frame. It is an object or dummy whose name picks when it shines; what you see comes from a BILLBOARD beneath it that the game switches on and off, and optionally a texture projected onto the road at night.

LIGHTIN*Turn indicatorsBlinking while the turn signal on its side is on. AI drivers signal their turns.211 models

Side comes from position, not from the name

For LIGHTIN, LIGHTBI and LIGHTLI the side is where the frame sits in the car: positive X is right, zero or negative is left. There is no L or R in these names — the number after the prefix is free. All of them take objects or dummies, and all of them are dark unless the camera is within the game’s activity range.

BILLBOARD*

Any typeBeneath a light

The first frame starting with BILLBOARD anywhere beneath a light frame is its visible glow, shown only while the light is lit. Shipped glows are objects with no_fog, sometimes real billboards with BBRD=z!.

Shipped: 354 models · 1,769 glows

PROJECTOR* with two LIGHT*

DummyOptional

A pair of headlights that cast one shared pool of light, with a picture that changes as they break. The dummy needs a three-texture property and exactly two frames starting with LIGHT beneath it; both must use one of the six light names and have no texture of their own. The pool is cast along the dummy’s facing.

Shipped: 348 models, each holding two LIGHTRE

What every light shares

  • Ground pool. A [texture] on the light frame projects that picture onto the road — only at night or in a tunnel, and only while the camera is near. For LIGHTRE it is aimed straight ahead along the car; other lights aim along their own facing.
  • Real light. A car the player is sitting in also gets a warm spot light (25–60 range) at each projecting light.
  • Breaking. A light is broken when the [DEFORM LIGHT] part it is, or the nearest deformable part above it, gives way. A broken light stays dark until the car is repaired.

Exhaust, fuel and AI points

EXHAUST*

Dummy or objectOptionalRemoved

Where exhaust smoke comes out, and in which direction (its facing). Any number of them. Child of BODY.

Shipped: 381 models · mostly a single EXHAUST

SMOKE_MOTOR

DummyOptionalRemoved

Where smoke rises from a damaged engine. Child of BODY; without it the smoke comes from the car’s origin.

Shipped: 312 models

FUEL

DummyOptionalRemoved

The filler cap. At a gas station the attendant lines the car up so this point sits by the pump. Measured from the model’s origin, so keep it at the top level rather than under BODY.

Shipped: 359 models · 12 of them under BODY

AI00 · AI01

DummyRequiredRemoved

Two reference points, normally front and rear, that the AI measures the car from: spacing traffic when it spawns, spotting what the car is about to hit, and the direction a character is pushed out when forced from the car. The first one’s height also sets how high the AI’s outline of the car sits. Children of BODY. Exact names — AI0 or AI000 do not count.

Shipped: 388 models

BALLAST*

DummyOptionalRemoved

Extra weight points, looked for only beneath BODY and sorted by the number after the name. They count alongside the seats when the game shifts the centre of mass for the weight on board.

Shipped: none
The file name matters too

The game reads a car’s name from its file without the last two characters before the extension (polimFor00.4ds → POLIMFOR). A car named POLCAD, POLI, POLIMFOR or POLIMTUD this way is treated as a police car.

Dummy01 … Dummy04

DummyOptional

Up to four collision boxes, from each dummy’s bounds. Exactly these four names; a non-dummy with one of them is skipped.

Part two · User properties

What “read” means here

The game never interprets user properties in general. A handful of places look at the text of specific frames, each in its own way — some want the whole text to match, some search for a word anywhere, some take a single character at a fixed position. Unless an entry says otherwise, case matters and the text can hold other things around it.

Characters

A character is a skinned mesh with joints under it. The game looks up all of these by name, and every one of them is a string in the game’s own program.

base

Single Mesh or Single MorphRequired

The character’s body. The animations the game ships move the body through this frame and only turn the joints, so a skinned mesh under any other name has its limbs bent by an animation while the body itself stays where it stood — through a crouch or a roll it never leaves the spot. The frame also carries the body’s collision sphere, the hit skeleton and the death points.

Of the game’s 322 skinned meshes, 313 are called base. The nine others are birds called Base, dogs called a, and three custom characters — each played only by animations made for it, keyed to its own name.

Shipped: 293 framesWhere it rests: hip height

back1 · neck · l_thigh …

Joint

The eighteen joints of the human skeleton, hanging under base: back1, back2, back3, neck, l_shoulder, l_arm, l_elbow, l_hand and the same four on the right, l_thigh, l_shin, l_foot and the same three on the right. The game’s character animations name these joints, so a skeleton that spells one differently is not turned by them. The add-on’s character preset builds exactly this set.

Shipped: 284 characters carry the full setCeiling: 64 joints under a mesh

gun1 · gun2

Dummy

Where a weapon sits in the hand: gun1 the right hand, gun2 the left. The weapon model is hung on the frame, so its place and facing decide how the gun is held.

Shipped: 270 each

notify

Dummy

Where an animation’s event cues land — footsteps and the like. The game only reads cues from a track of this name, so cues keyed anywhere else are never played. Every one of the 642 animations the game ships puts them here.

Shipped: 279 characters

blnd

Dummy

Moved by the animations themselves, which use it to join one animation onto the next — the body carries on from where the last one left it instead of snapping back.

Shipped: 261 characters

targetN · targetE

Target

What the head turns toward. A target frame lists the frames that follow it, and on a character that is the neck; the game moves the target and the head tracks it. targetN is the one nearly every character carries; targetE appears on a handful.

Shipped: 178 and 10

Named by a mission, not by the game

These names appear in no game program at all. The mission’s own script names them in scene2.bin and acts on them there, so they mean whatever that mission asked of them. They are listed because they look like engine names and are not.

Names nothing reads

Most dummies in the game’s models are named by whoever modeled them. What the game acts on there is the frame’s user property, not its name — a collision volume is VOLB in the text, whatever the frame is called. Rename these freely.

Dummy

Kolize is Czech for collision, and it is the usual name for a collision dummy: kol 1, kolize01, ~~kol01, koldes, kolpult. The leading ~~ is a sorting trick in the modeling tool, not anything the game sees.

What the modeling tool names a new object. Over four thousand frames in the game’s models still carry one, on everything from collision boxes to lamp posts.

Known to the game, never used

The game looks these up, and no model it ships answers. They are the safest names to try if you want behavior nothing else is using — and the likeliest to have been dropped late.

BALLAST*

DummyNever used

Looked for under BODY on a vehicle, beside the seats and the collision dummies. Not one of the 440 shipped vehicles carries one. See Exhaust, fuel and AI points for what the game does with it.

LIGHTBA*

DummyNever used

One of the vehicle light families, in the table beside LIGHTBR*, LIGHTBI*, LIGHTIN* and LIGHTRE*, which are all used. Nothing shipped has a LIGHTBA light.
