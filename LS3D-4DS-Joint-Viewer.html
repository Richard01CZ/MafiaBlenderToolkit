Joint viewer

Open any 4DS model to see its joints. Each joint carries sixteen numbers describing a box in its own space: its area of influence, the region its skin weights were made from. The boxes are drawn on the model with the skin boxes the game uses for bounds, the picked joint's weights are shown on its vertices, and the panel lists every value the joint holds and where it sits in the file. Nothing leaves your computer: the file is read in this page.

Joints

How the game uses a joint
Position, rotation and scale
Place the joint against the frame it hangs from, the way every frame is placed. Everything hanging below it follows, and a skinned mesh bends with it: each of the mesh's vertices is carried by its joint's current placement through that joint's bind matrix in the skin.
Culling byte
Its lowest bit switches the joint on; switched off, nothing hanging below it is drawn. Its highest bit locks the joint's position. The six between are rebuilt when the model loads.
Name
The game finds joints by name: a character's hands, for instance, to put a weapon in them.
User properties
Kept with the frame, as on any frame.
The sixteen numbers
The joint's area of influence: a box in its own space, the first three rows its axes, each running from the center to the middle of a face, and the fourth where its center sits; the last column is always 0, 0, 0, 1. It is what the skin weights were made from when the model was exported. The third row runs along the bone, toward the joint's end of the limb: a vertex inside the box is shared between the joint and its parent by where it sits along that axis — none of it the joint's at the back face, all of it at the front — and the vertices past the front face belong to the joint alone. Every one of the 83,347 blended vertices in the game's 289 skinned models sits inside its joint's box with exactly that weight. The game itself loads the numbers and copies them when it duplicates a joint, and reads them for nothing else: it plays the weights, not the box. A joint the game makes itself gets a cube two centimeters across.
Joint number
A skinned mesh gathers the joints hanging below it, however deep — first child first, in the order the file lists them, at most 64 — and puts each in the slot its number names, counting from 0. Slot N is moved by the skin's group N, so the numbers have to run from 0 up with none missing or repeated. A joint not below a skinned mesh keeps a number nobody reads.
Bounds
A skinned mesh's bounds are its skin's group boxes, one per slot, each carried by its joint as it moves. Tick Skin group boxes to see them.
What the add-on does with each value
Position, rotation and scale
Become the bone and its joint scale. The export writes the values that put each bone exactly where it is, so a model that goes round unchanged comes back the same.
Culling byte and user properties
Kept on the bone and editable in its joint panel.
Name
The bone's name.
Joint number
Worked out on export from the order of the skinned mesh's vertex groups, one skin group per joint below the mesh. The export refuses more than 64 joints below one skinned mesh.
The sixteen numbers
The joint's influence box, whose local +Y is the axis the weight runs along. In the add-on it is kept on the joint itself - sixteen numbers on its pose bone, drawn over the bone and dragged by the handles on it - so a box nobody touches is written back to the bit. A joint made in Blender gets the box the game's own tool gave a new joint, to the last digit.
Weights
A joint with weights painted exports them as they are. A joint with none has them made from its box, the way the game's exporter made them: the vertices inside are shared with the joint's parent by where they sit along the box's axis, and the mesh ahead of the box, as far as it runs unbroken, is the joint's alone. On the game's characters that gives back 96.4% of their weights exactly.
